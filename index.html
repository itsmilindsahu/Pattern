Problem
01

Every system produces a score.
Ratio produces a decision.

The cost ratio r = Clate/Cearly was never made explicit per loan type, making threshold calibration arbitrary. Existing Early Warning Systems were built by score-only thinking — answering "is this customer risky?" rather than "is now the right time to act?"

Ratio's underpinning is the Optimal Stopping Problem — the same family of mathematics that powers sequential hypothesis testing, clinical trial stopping rules, and optimal asset liquidation. Applied to retail banking, it produces a time-varying boundary that calibrates urgency automatically by loan economics.

01

Pre-NPA Slippage

A retail loan crosses SMA-0 → SMA-1 → NPA. The moment it becomes NPA, interest recognition stops and mandatory provisioning begins — 15% for substandard, up to 100% for loss assets. On a ₹5L personal loan: ₹75,000 in provisioning and ₹18,000 in lost annual interest.

02 — The Framework

Optimal stopping,
not classification.

Ratio does not replace your EWS. It converts scores into timed, costed, prioritised action lists. The stopping boundary shrinks as remaining loan tenure decreases, encoding the increasing urgency of intervention as the account approaches maturity. When Clate ≫ Cearly, the boundary drops — you intervene at the first whisper of stress.

Time-varying stopping boundary

n      — sequential observations to date
